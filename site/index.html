04Craft

Nothing is added.
Only removed.

A monolithic bath begins as several tonnes of rock and ends as a vessel you can sit inside. Between those two facts there is no casting, no mould, no joint — only subtraction. The work runs four to twenty weeks depending on the hardness of the stone, some of it machine-assisted, most of it by hand. A block can be lost at any stage — a hidden fissure, a vein that runs wrong — and the work begins again.

01

Chosen at the face

Blocks are chosen at the quarry face, not from a yard. We look for sound stone: dense, dry, free of the hairline fractures that open under a chisel.

02

The block is read

Wet, the mass is studied for hours. Veining, translucency and the fall of the grain decide where the rim will sit and which face meets the light.

03

Hollowed by machine and hand

A machine-guided diamond head takes out the bulk under constant water. Everything after is chisel and rasp — the interior curve, the lip, the radius a shoulder will find.

04

Honed, not polished

Eight grades of abrasive, wet-sanded by hand from coarse to fine. The finish is not a polish but a hone — matte, faintly warm, closer to skin than to glass.

05

Sealed, then left to cure

An impregnating sealer is drawn into the pores, not laid on top. The piece then rests: days of curing before it is allowed to hold water.

06

Filled, inspected, crated, shipped

Every vessel is filled and stood overnight, checked for seepage and level, then foam-blocked into a timber frame built to its dimensions and released to sea freight.

Our masons

Earth Made does not own a quarry, and does not claim to. We work with a small number of stone workshops, chosen over years for one reason: they still carve monolithic vessels by hand, and they will carve to our drawings rather than to their own catalogue. Each house keeps its own masters and its own methods; our part is the design, the stone, the tolerances, and the refusal to accept a piece that misses them. We name the material and the stone on every vessel. We do not name the ateliers — the ordinary discretion any house extends to the people who make its work.

Every vessel is water-tested and inspected before it is crated, and carries a ten-year structural guarantee: if the stone fails, we replace the piece.

Made to order

Nothing waits in a warehouse. When you reserve, a block is selected for you and cut — 4 to 20 weeks at the bench depending on the stone and the form. Your dates are confirmed in writing once your block is chosen, not before.

Payment

A reservation is not a charge. A secure payment link follows within 24 hours: 30% to begin carving, the balance due before dispatch. You may cancel for any reason and be refunded in full within 72 hours of your deposit clearing. After that window your block is bought, committed and photographed for you, cutting begins, and the commission cannot be cancelled and the deposit is not returnable. The deposit represents identified costs incurred at that moment — acquiring and reserving your block, and holding bench time against it; if the committed cost proves materially lower, we refund you the difference. None of this affects your rights if a piece arrives late, damaged, faulty or not as described.

Freight & insurance

We crate, book and manage the shipment door to door, and insure it in transit to full declared value. A fixed regional figure — $1,900 to $3,400 — is quoted with your reservation and paid by the client.

Duties & import taxes

Every piece is supplied DAP — Delivered At Place: you are the importer of record, and import duty, customs clearance and destination VAT are assessed by your own authority and paid by you. These are not small sums and we will never let them surprise you. Into the EU expect duty of about 1.7% plus import VAT at your national rate — 17% to 27%, charged on the vessel, the freight and the insurance together; into the UK, 20%. No payment link is ever sent until you have received, in writing, our estimate of these charges for your own country and told us you have read it.

Delivery

White-glove delivery to your threshold, unpacked and the the piece inspected with you before anything is signed. We leave all packaging with you until you confirm the vessel is accepted — it is the evidence any transit claim depends on. Plumbing is connected by your own licensed installer to rough-in drawings issued at reservation. Setting a piece above the ground floor is rigging work — we quote it with a local specialist or coordinate with your builder.

Care & guarantee

Sealed before dispatch. Clean with pH-neutral soap and warm water; re-seal every 12 to 18 months. Every vessel is water-tested and photographed before it leaves the bench, and carries a ten-year structural guarantee.

Questions we are asked

Is it really carved from one solid block?

Yes — that is the definition of the collection. Each vessel is bored, hollowed, shaped and honed from one piece of stone: no resin core, no stone veneer over a substrate, no seam and no join in the body. One exception is stated openly. Aurora is not monolithic, and we will not pretend otherwise: rose quartz does not occur in sound masses of this size — a genuinely single-block rose quartz bath is a museum object priced in seven figures. Aurora is natural rose quartz, hand-selected and hand-set over a solid stone core. We confirm the substrate, the bonding system and the finished weight to you in writing, with photographs, before anything is committed.

It is the reason a piece weighs what it does, holds the heat of a bath the way it does, and takes between fourteen and thirty-two weeks to reach you.

What does it weigh — and will my floor carry it?

Empty weights across the twelve run from 300 kg for the onyx vessels to 1,315 kg for Erebus. Filled, with a bather in it, add roughly 300 kg.

On a ground floor or a concrete slab this is rarely a question. On a suspended timber floor, or anywhere above the ground storey, ask your architect or a structural engineer to confirm the loading before you reserve. We will send the exact weight, footprint and load-spread figures for your piece at no charge — we would far rather you checked than guessed.

How does it connect to plumbing — and do you install it?

The waste outlet is cored and a pop-up waste fitted before dispatch. There is no tap on the vessel itself: the filler is floor- or wall-mounted and chosen by you, which is why rough-in drawings and the waste position are issued at reservation, well before a bathroom is closed up. The connection is made by your own licensed plumber working to those drawings.

White-glove delivery brings the crate to your threshold, uncrates it and leaves every part of the packaging with you until you confirm acceptance in writing — a transit claim cannot be defended on a crate that went back on the truck. Moving several hundred kilos of stone through a finished house is rigging work rather than delivery — tell us where the piece should stand and we will quote it with a specialist in your city, or coordinate with your builder while the room is still open.

How long does it really take?

Fourteen to twenty-two weeks from reservation for most of the collection. Twenty-four to thirty-two for Aurora and Lignum, whose material genuinely cannot be rushed. Roughly half of that is carving and finishing; the rest is ocean freight and clearance.

Air freight removes about four weeks from the shipping leg and is quoted on request. We confirm your dates in writing once your block is selected, and we would rather tell you a true date late than a comfortable one early.

What happens if it chips or cracks?

In transit, nothing is at your risk. The shipment is insured to full declared value; if a piece is damaged in transit we handle the claim and the replacement, and you deal only with us.

In the house, small edge chips in natural stone are repairable in place — a colour-matched repair kit ships with every vessel, and we can introduce you to a stone restorer in your city. Every piece carries a ten-year structural guarantee against through-cracking in normal domestic use. Natural fissures, colour movement and vein lines are characteristics of the material rather than faults; they are the reason you chose stone.

Can I choose the stone — or commission something else entirely?

You can. Each of the twelve is shown in the stone the form was drawn for, but most can be cut from an alternative block. Say so at reservation and we will send photographs of what is available and re-quote if the material changes the price.
